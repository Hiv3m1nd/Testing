# Year by Year — Episode "1 AD": research dossier

This file records every factual claim made in the narration or on screen, what
kind of claim it is, the sources consulted, and the known uncertainties. It
also flags the places where the picture is an illustration or reconstruction
rather than evidence.

**Status key**

- **DATED 1 AD:** an event securely dated to the year AD 1.
- **CONTEXT:** a true state of affairs in AD 1 that is not a dated event.
- **ESTIMATE:** a modern scholarly estimate; a range is given.
- **TRADITION:** a source's own claim or a traditional story, attributed as such.
- **SETUP:** an event of AD 2 that sets up the next episode.

Research note: for this session, the sandbox's network policy blocked direct
page fetches (Wikipedia, museum APIs and similar). The claims were checked with
web search across multiple independent results, and the primary texts are cited
by standard reference. Before a public release, anyone can re-check the
starred (*) items against the primary text in a library or a translation.

## 2. Narrative choice

The episode has one spine: **"Year One" is a label applied afterwards**. In
AD 1 itself, the two great empires at either end of Eurasia were both quietly
preoccupied with succession.

- **Rome:** an ageing Augustus and two young heirs. Julia is in exile and
  Tiberius is shut out on Rhodes.
- **China:** a boy emperor and an ambitious regent. Wang Mang's white-pheasant
  omen is *the* securely dated political event of AD 1 in China, and the start
  of a long series arc (usurpation in AD 9, the Xin dynasty, its fall in AD 23).
- **Between them:** Parthia (Musa), and the silk and monsoon trade that
  physically linked the two ends.

I deliberately left out a "tour of every civilization". Teotihuacan, the Maya
(El Mirador), Kush (Meroë), Germania (the road to Teutoburg, AD 9), India and
the steppe are context the series can pick up as their stories become
eventful. Nothing in the episode is invented to make the year more exciting.

### Series hooks planted

| Thread | Introduced | Pays off |
|---|---|---|
| Augustus's succession: Gaius, Lucius, Tiberius, Julia | rome | Lucius dies AD 2; Gaius dies AD 4; Tiberius adopted AD 4; Augustus dies AD 14 |
| Wang Mang | china / pheasant | Daughter married to Emperor Ping AD 4; Ping dies AD 6; Xin dynasty AD 9; Yellow River shift AD 11; fall AD 23 |
| Parthia, Armenia, Musa | east | Gaius meets Phraataces on the Euphrates (AD 2); Musa and her son deposed (c. AD 4) |
| Silk and the monsoon trade | trade | Roman complaints about silk; Gan Ying's mission west (AD 97) |
| Han census of AD 2 | next | Episode 2 AD |

## 3. Claims, line by line

### Cold open and calendar

1. **"No one alive in 1 AD knew it was 1 AD."** CONTEXT. The Anno Domini era
   was devised by Dionysius Exiguus in **AD 525** for his Easter tables.
   Contemporaries dated years by consuls (Rome) or by reign-era names (China).
   *Sources:* "Anno Domini" (Wikipedia); obliquity.com/calendar/ad.html;
   "Year zero" (Wikipedia).
2. **"invented five centuries later, by a monk named Dionysius Exiguus"**:
   AD 525 (≈ 524 years later). He was a monk living in Rome. On screen: *ANNI
   DOMINI NOSTRI IESV CHRISTI*, the wording he used for his year count.
3. **"He counted from the birth of Jesus, and probably got it wrong."**
   CONTEXT/ESTIMATE. Dionysius never explains how he fixed his epoch.
   Historians agree the Nativity cannot fall later than Herod's death.
   *Uncertainty:* strictly, he counted from the Incarnation, and he doesn't say
   whether he meant the conception or the birth. The narration simplifies this
   to "birth".
4. **"Most historians place that birth a few years earlier, before King Herod
   died, around 4 BC."** ESTIMATE. The common scholarly range for the birth is
   **c. 6–4 BC** (shown on screen as "most estimates"). Herod's death is
   traditionally dated to 4 BC; a minority argues for 1 BC. The episode uses
   "around 4 BC" and never says Jesus was born in AD 1.
5. **"There is no year zero. 1 BC runs straight into AD 1."** CONTEXT. Bede
   (8th century) popularised BC dating, and the system has no year 0.

### What people called the year

6. **Rome: "the year that Gaius Caesar and Lucius Aemilius Paullus were
   consuls."** DATED 1 AD. They were the *consules ordinarii* of AD 1.
   *Sources:* livius.org (Gaius Caesar); the GRBS article on Gaius's eastern
   mission ("he assumed his consulate for A.D. 1").
   - *On screen:* a carved inscription reading **C·CAESARE / L·AEMILIO·PAVLLO /
     COS**. **This is an illustration** written in the standard Latin consular
     dating formula. It is **not** a facsimile of a particular surviving
     stone. The red-painted letters reflect the common Roman practice of
     rubricating inscriptions.
7. **China: "a new era, called Yuanshi (元始), the Original Beginning."**
   DATED 1 AD. Emperor Ping's era Yuanshi ran from AD 1 to 5; Yuanshi year 1
   is AD 1. 元始 means roughly "the original/primordial beginning". Strictly,
   every new era name restarted the count at year one; the coincidence lies in
   the *meaning* of this particular name. *Sources:* "Yuanshi" (Wikipedia);
   chinesenotes.com (元始); "List of Chinese era names".
   On screen: the Han dynasty ruling "since 202 BC" (standard dating).

### Two empires

8. **"opposite ends of Eurasia, eight thousand kilometres apart."** Computed:
   the great-circle distance from Rome (41.89 N, 12.49 E) to Han Chang'an
   (34.31 N, 108.86 E) is **8,013 km**.
9. **"perhaps a hundred million people. A third or more of humanity."**
   ESTIMATE.
   - Roman Empire under Augustus: about **45–60 million** (Frier ≈ 46 M;
     Beloch 54 M in AD 14; higher counts exist).
   - Han: the AD 2 census recorded **57.7–59.6 million** people (see item 20).
   - World, AD 1: **≈ 170–300 million**. McEvedy & Jones give ≈ 169 M, Maddison
     ≈ 231 M, and other compilations go higher.
   - The two empires together held roughly 100–120 M, so 33–60 % of the world
     depending on the estimate. That's why the narration says "perhaps" and "a
     third or more".
   - *Sources:* "Estimates of historical world population" (Wikipedia);
     tabulae-geographicae.de/2017/06/06/population-numbers; Scheidel, "Roman
     population size" (PDF); ancient-history-sites.com.
10. **"To Romans, the Chinese were the Seres: the silk people."** CONTEXT.
    Virgil's *Georgics* 2.121 (c. 29 BC) mentions the Seres combing silk "from
    the leaves". The Greek and Latin *Seres* is connected with the word for
    silk. Contact was indirect, through middlemen. *Sources:* Gibbon (ccel.org)
    citing Virgil; UNH Inquiry Journal, "Tales of the Seres and Silk" (2025).

### Rome

11. **"Augustus, was in his sixties"**: born 23 September 63 BC, so 63–64 in
    AD 1. On screen: "born 63 BC · in power since 31 BC" (Actium).
12. **"His heirs were his grandsons, Gaius and Lucius, adopted as his sons."**
    CONTEXT. They were the sons of Julia and Agrippa, born 20 BC and 17 BC,
    and adopted by Augustus in 17 BC.
    - *On screen:* a reconstruction of the **Gaius & Lucius denarius** (RIC I
      207, Lugdunum mint). Its reverse shows the two princes standing with
      shields and spears, a simpulum and lituus above, the exergue *C L
      CAESARES*, and the legend *AVGVSTI F COS DESIG PRINC IVVENT*.
    - The issue is usually dated **2 BC – AD 4**, so it was being struck in
      AD 1. The coin in the film is drawn procedurally after this type. It is
      not a photograph.
13. **"Their mother, his daughter Julia, had been banished to a tiny island."**
    CONTEXT. She was exiled to **Pandateria** (Ventotene) in **2 BC**.
    *Sources:* worldhistory.org; imperiumromanum.pl; ancientromelive.org.
14. **"his stepson Tiberius had withdrawn to Rhodes, and was refused
    permission to come home."** CONTEXT. Tiberius retired to Rhodes in 6 BC;
    his requests to return were refused, and he came back to Rome only in
    AD 2 (Suetonius, *Tiberius* 10–13).
    *Uncertainty:* one popular site gives a garbled "2 BC" recall; that is an
    error.

### The East

15. **"This year, Gaius, barely twenty, was consul, and far to the east,
    facing Rome's great rival: Parthia."** DATED 1 AD (the consulship) plus
    CONTEXT (the mission). Gaius held the consulship of AD 1 while on his
    eastern command over Armenia and Parthia.
    - His famous **summit with the Parthian king on an island in the
      Euphrates** is dated **AD 2** by most scholars (livius.org says AD 1).
      Velleius Paterculus was an eyewitness (2.101). The episode **does not**
      show the summit; it's held for episode 2 AD.
16. **"Its young king ruled beside his mother, Musa."** CONTEXT. Phraates V
    (Phraataces, born c. 19 BC) reigned 2 BC – AD 4 with his mother Musa.
    *Sources:* "Phraates V" (Wikipedia); coinweek.com (Parthian coinage).
17. **"The historian Josephus says she had arrived as an Italian slave girl: a
    gift from Augustus himself."** TRADITION, attributed. Josephus,
    *Antiquities* 18.39–43*, says Augustus sent Phraates IV an Italian
    slave-girl among other gifts. Modern accounts tie this to c. 20 BC.
    - Josephus also says she and her son killed Phraates IV, and that she
      married her son. The episode does **not** repeat the second claim; it is
      contested.
18. **"Now her face was on Parthia's coins."** CONTEXT. Musa is the only
    Parthian queen shown and named on coins. The legend reads ΘΕΑΣ ΟΥΡΑΝΙΑΣ
    ΜΟΥΣΗΣ ΒΑΣΙΛΙΣΣΗΣ ("of Queen Musa, heavenly goddess"); on many specimens
    it is blundered.
    - *On screen:* a procedural reconstruction after her drachm type (bust
      left, tall jewelled tiara). It is stylised, **not a portrait**.

### Trade

19. **"Chinese silk moved west, from trader to trader."** CONTEXT. No
    individual is known to have travelled the whole route; goods passed
    through Central Asian and Parthian middlemen.
    - *On screen:* the route is a schematic of the main caravan line: Chang'an
      → Hexi Corridor → Dunhuang → Tarim → Merv → Ctesiphon → Palmyra →
      Antioch.
    - The name "Silk Road" is modern (19th century), so the narration says
      "silk trade" instead.
20. **"From Roman Egypt, ships rode the monsoon winds to India. Strabo heard of
    as many as a hundred and twenty a year."** CONTEXT. Strabo,
    *Geography* 2.5.12: when he travelled up the Nile with the prefect Aelius
    Gallus (c. 26–24 BC), up to 120 ships were sailing from **Myos Hormos** to
    India. On screen: "from one Egyptian port". *Sources:* "Myos Hormos" and
    "Indo-Roman trade relations" (Wikipedia); Blue 2007 (ancientportsantiques
    PDF).

### China

21. **"Chang'an, capital of the Han."** 長安 means "Lasting/Perpetual Peace".
22. **"Its emperor was a boy of about nine."** Emperor Ping (平帝), born 9 BC,
    reigned 1 BC – AD 6.
23. **"Real power lay with his regent: Wang Mang."** CONTEXT. In 1 BC the Grand
    Empress Dowager Wang Zhengjun (his aunt) recalled Wang Mang as regent.
    On screen, "aged about 45": Wang Mang was born c. 45 BC.
24. **"Early this year, envoys from the far south presented a rare white
    pheasant."** DATED 1 AD.
    - *Hanshu* 99A (Biography of Wang Mang) records that in the first month of
      Yuanshi 1 a white pheasant was offered at the ancestral temples.
    - Other Han records: the Yuechang (越裳氏), through interpreters, presented
      one white and two black pheasants (越裳氏重譯獻白雉一、黑雉二).
    - *Sources:* zh.wikisource 漢書/卷099上; 前漢紀 (Han Ji) 卷三十; Thomsen and
      the Cambridge *Early China* article "Wang Mang (c. 45 BCE – 23 CE) and
      Classical Learning as Path to Supreme Power"; leminhkhai.blog, "A white
      pheasant and the Sino-Vietnamese tributary relationship".
25. **"Such a bird was said to have once honoured the Duke of Zhou, the
    legendary regent of a boy king."** TRADITION. In the classical tradition,
    the Yuechang brought a white pheasant to the Zhou court while the Duke of
    Zhou was regent for the young King Cheng (≈ 11th century BC).
    - The ministers in AD 1 explicitly invoked "the white-pheasant omen of
      [King] Cheng of Zhou" (周成白雉之瑞). That comparison was the whole
      point: Wang Mang as a new Duke of Zhou.
26. **"Wang Mang was named Duke Who Secures the Han."** DATED 1 AD. The title
    is 安漢公 (Ānhàn gōng), also translated "Duke Who Pacifies/Gives Peace to
    the Han". *Source:* *Hanshu* 99A (宜賜號曰安漢公).
27. **"And, the official history notes, he had arranged the pheasant
    himself."** TRADITION, attributed. The Han records say the tribute was
    procured at Wang Mang's prompting through officials in Yizhou (莽令益州諷使之).
    Note that the *Hanshu* was written under the restored Han dynasty and is
    hostile to Wang Mang.

### The close and the setup

28. **"In Rome, an ageing emperor and two young heirs. In China, a boy emperor
    and an ambitious regent."** A summary of 11–14 and 22–23.
29. **"Next year, the Han will count their people: nearly sixty million."**
    SETUP (AD 2). The census of Yuanshi 2 (AD 2), preserved in the *Hanshu*
    geography treatise, records 12.2–12.4 million households and **57,671,400**
    or **59,594,978** individuals; the totals differ between the summed
    commandery figures and the stated total. On screen: a dot grid and a
    counter to 59.6 M, with "nearly sixty million" in the narration.
    *Sources:* "Census in China" (Wikipedia); Seshat (Bielenstein 1987);
    historyofinformation.com.
30. **"And in Rome, one of the two heirs will not live to see the year's
    end."** SETUP (AD 2). Lucius Caesar died at Massilia on **20 August AD 2**,
    aged 18. The episode deliberately doesn't say which heir.
    *Source:* "Lucius Caesar" (Wikipedia; Hopwood, UNE).

## 4. Maps and other imagery

**Borders (`src/geo.py`)** are approximate reconstructions for AD 1, drawn by
hand from the standard historical outlines and then clipped to real coastlines
(Natural Earth). Specific choices:

- **Rome:** provinces in full colour; **client kingdoms** lighter (Mauretania,
  Thrace, Cappadocia, Pontus, Commagene, the Herodian territories, Nabataea,
  Bosporus). The area between the Rhine and the Elbe is not drawn as a province
  (Roman military control, 9 BC – AD 9). Moesia is shown as Roman-controlled,
  though its formal status as a province in AD 1 is debated.
- **Parthia:** the eastern frontier around Sistan and Arachosia is uncertain;
  shown at about 61–63° E. **Armenia** is hatched as contested.
- **Han:** Hainan is excluded (its commanderies were abandoned in 46 BC).
  Northwest Korea (Lelang) and northern Vietnam (to Rinan) are included.
  Central and eastern Yunnan (Yizhou) are included; Yongchang (AD 69) is not.
  The Tarim Basin (Protectorate of the Western Regions) is not shaded as part
  of the empire.
- **Rivers:** modern courses, except the **lower Yellow River**, which is drawn
  on its approximate pre-AD 11 northern course (it shifted south in AD 11,
  another future episode). The modern lower course is cut.
- **Water:** reservoirs (for example Lake Nasser) are removed. The **Aral Sea**
  is restored to its pre-1960 shoreline (53 m contour) and **Lake Texcoco** is
  refilled.
- **Terrain:** AWS Terrain Tiles / Mapzen Terrarium elevation, with a natural,
  pre-modern palette (no farmland, cities or roads). Desert and vegetation
  zones are painted approximately.
- **Night lights on the globe** are artistic firelight scattered over the
  densely settled regions. They are **not** population data.

**Cities and buildings**

- **Rome at dusk:** an impression, not a reconstruction. It shows temples on
  the Capitoline, insulae, umbrella pines and cypresses, and no domes. The
  foreground statue uses the raised-arm *adlocutio* pose known from the
  Augustus of Prima Porta.
- **Chang'an at dawn:** an impression of Han-period architecture: halls on
  stepped rammed-earth terraces, nearly straight-eaved hip roofs, paired *que*
  gate towers and round tile-ends. Later Ming/Qing-style upswept eaves are
  deliberately avoided.

**Remaining imagery**

- **The white pheasant** is a stylised illustration.
- **The Duke of Zhou** is evoked only by the characters 周公.
- **The oil lamps** are a symbol. No lamp or flame is a historical claim.
