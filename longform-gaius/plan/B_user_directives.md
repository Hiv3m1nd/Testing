The owner's script notes from 2026-10-15, on the Gaius film. v2 was "MUCH BETTER"; keep writing that way.

- **Tell it as a story, not a list of facts.**
  - Pick 2–3 big stories from the life and tell them in full, vivid detail. Fill the gaps between them with brief connective detail.
  - Fewer facts, told well, beats cramming.
  - The main character stays at the center. Family dynamics and relationships are what viewers hang on to.
- **Personal, never abstract.**
  - Never flatten people into roles. "An old man missed his grandson" was called robotic and distant.
  - Use names and specifics, and the person's own words where the sources have them.
- **Flow.**
  - No stop-start runs of short declaratives. Connect thoughts.
  - Avoid stiff constructions like "gave birth to a boy, and named him Gaius".
- **Clarity.**
  - Explain era terms in modern words: what a military standard is, what a magistrate is.
  - Make it obvious who did what to whom ("he bought them": bought whom?).
  - Lead with the plain meaning (adopted), then the colour (the bronze-and-scales ceremony).
- **Cut the extras.**
  - Drop side characters who appear for one line. Name the eyewitness historian only if it matters.
  - Drop asides that don't serve the story, such as the Parthian king's mother.
- **Implication over hedging where it's still accurate.** Example: "the king revealed Lollius's betrayal… a few days later, Lollius was dead", without "Velleius can't say if it was accident or suicide".
- **Show, don't tell.** Example: "he did what his grandfather asked", not "against his will".
- **Reveals must be real and clearly set up.** The "empty tomb, it was a cenotaph" twist confused him, so it was dropped. Anything raised in the hook must pay off cleanly.
- **Endings land on human meaning.** "A little peace", not "to be allowed to stop". The story needs a clean ending.
- **Show notes in parentheses** in scripts, for visuals and music:
  - show what's named (the real Ara Pacis, the ruins);
  - make the visuals worked and beautiful, never a pasted image;
  - make big moments intense.
- **Never put the owner's instructions into the narration** (no "people want…" meta talk).

**Why:** the long-form must be dramatic, gripping and narrative-driven, clearly better than the shorts.

**How to apply:** for every long-form script and rewrite. Related: [[year-by-year-longform]].
